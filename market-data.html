AIAuditTrail.com Market Data

Documented AI failures rose 55% in a single year while the market for governing AI is still small enough to measure in hundreds of millions. That tension is the whole story.

The Stanford AI Index has become the closest thing the AI industry has to an official scoreboard, and its incident count is moving in the wrong direction. The AI Incident Database recorded 362 documented incidents in 2025, up from 233 in 2024 — harms and near-harms from deployed systems, from hiring discrimination to deepfake fraud to medical AI errors. The 55% jump tracks adoption: more systems in production means more failure modes, and governance frameworks are still catching up to deployment speed.

The money side tells a similar story in miniature. Published estimates for the AI governance market in 2024 range from $0.89 billion (MarketsandMarkets) to $1.0 billion (Global Industry Analysts), but their forecasts diverge sharply — $5.78 billion by 2029 versus $17.0 billion by 2030. The disagreement is definitional: whether services, consulting, and adjacent compliance tooling count. What both agree on is direction and speed, with growth rates above 45% a year.

Regulation is where the costs stop being theoretical. The EU AI Act, in force since August 2024 with phased application through 2028, sets fines up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices. NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, published in January 2023, carries no fines at all — it is voluntary — but its Govern, Map, Measure, Manage structure has become the working vocabulary for firms building audit trails before a regulator asks for one.

Reading the numbers

The incident count is a floor, not a total. The AI Incident Database records documented cases; unreported failures, internal near-misses, and harms nobody linked to a model never enter it. A rising count therefore proves rising documented harm and rising detection at the same time, and the two cannot be separated from the headline alone.

The market forecasts should be read as a range with a wide error bar. When two reputable firms differ threefold on a 2030 total, the honest statement is that the market is under $1 billion now and growing fast — not that it will be any particular number in 2030.

Methodology

Included: public press-release and summary market sizes (two scopes, both labeled), Stanford AI Index incident counts as reported in public summaries of the Index, EU AI Act penalty ceilings as cited in public compliance writing, and the NIST framework publication date from NIST's own PDF. Excluded: paywalled market reports, OECD incident statistics that could not be verified to a public page in this session, and any compliance-cost estimate without a named public source.
